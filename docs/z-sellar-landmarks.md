# Sellar and clinoid source landmarks

This source guide includes historical teaching-validation records. Curricula, lessons and course workflows described here are retained in `codex/release-testing`; the formal release exposes the same source anatomy through [Browse anatomy](getting-started.md).

The `z-anatomy-sellar-landmarks` curriculum provides eighteen views of nine original author locations on the Z-Anatomy sphenoid bone. It reuses the accepted native source model, its original geometry and the existing source-annotation adapter. Each location has a complete bone-context view and a focused view of the same point.

The locations are the sella turcica, hypophysial fossa, tuberculum sellae, dorsum sellae, anterior clinoid process, source-labeled `(Middle clinoid process)`, posterior clinoid process, carotid sulcus and chiasmatic sulcus. The parentheses around the middle clinoid name belong to the source label. This source-model example is retained as delivered; an individual normal-variant case requires separate source evidence.

All nine annotations are original two-vertex author label lines. Their active Hook modifiers attach vertex 1 to the original `Sphenoid bone` object, and their text-object parent hierarchy identifies the displayed source label. Preparation evaluates that endpoint in the pinned Blender runtime with embedded source scripts disabled, then retains the original world transform and metric scale. Each portable scene includes the complete source-annotation record and the exact atlas binding. A point annotation and its bony context remain distinct representations.

## Guided anatomical reading

Start with the complete sphenoid view, identify the lesser and greater wings, then follow the paired views through the sellar landmarks. Describe the visible ridges and depressions relative to the original marker. Record observations in the scene note and return to the recorded view between comparisons.

The anterior clinoid belongs to the lesser wing. The optic strut connects its base to the sphenoid body, forms the inferior boundary of the optic canal and separates that canal from the superior orbital fissure. These relationships provide a reading framework for rotating the source bone. The accompanying anatomical reference describes the bony connections and their normal variation: [Tayebi Meybodi et al., 2023, osseous and meningeal anatomy of the middle cranial fossa](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/surgery/articles/10.3389/fsurg.2023.1132774/full).

A study of 200 dry skulls documents variation in anterior-clinoid shape, optic-strut attachment and bony connections between anterior and middle clinoids. Use its original specimen observations to discuss how a source-model example relates to individual anatomy: [Kapur and Mehic, 2012, anatomical variations of the optic strut and anterior clinoid process](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4362444/). This curriculum's original points remain tied to the Z-Anatomy model; individual variant examples can be added through separately documented sources.
